Run your loan numbers

Deal breakdown: the “10%” loan that costs 15% (EXAMPLE numbers)

These are made-up numbers to show the math. They aren't from a real property or any lender.

Buy for $160,000, $40,000 rehab, $260,000 ARV. The lender covers 90% of purchase ($144,000) and all of the rehab ($40,000), so the loan is $184,000. Terms: 10% interest-only, 2 points, $1,500 in fees, $250 per draw. Rehab money comes out in 4 equal draws, one at the end of each of the first 4 months, and you sell in month 7.

Cost Interest on draws only Interest on full loan
Interest over 7 months $9,900 $10,733
Points (2% of $184,000) $3,680 $3,680
Lender fees $1,500 $1,500
Draw fees (4 × $250) $1,000 $1,000
Total loan cost $16,080 $16,913
True yearly cost 15.0% 15.8%

How we got the interest: the $144,000 purchase part is out all 7 months, so $144,000 × 10% ÷ 12 × 7 = $8,400. Each $10,000 draw is only out for 6, 5, 4, and 3 months, which adds $1,500. Total $9,900. If the lender charges on the full $184,000 from day one, it's $184,000 × 10% ÷ 12 × 7 = $10,733. That's $833 for money you haven't even touched yet.

True yearly cost is total cost ÷ loan ÷ 7 months × 12. So $16,080 ÷ $184,000 is 8.7% over 7 months, or about 15.0% a year.

Now say the project runs 3 months long and you sell in month 10. Interest climbs to $14,500 and the total to $20,680 (still on the draws-only version, and before any extension fee). That's $4,600 more out of your profit.

The rate is the number on the ad. The total is the number that comes out of your check. Run both before you sign.

Quick tip: ask "Dutch or non-Dutch?"

"Dutch" interest means you pay on the whole loan, including rehab money the lender is still holding. "Non-Dutch" means you only pay on what's been drawn. Lenders don't always put it on the first quote, so ask, and get the answer in writing next to the rate, points, and every fee.
