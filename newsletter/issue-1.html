Deal breakdown (EXAMPLE numbers, not a real property)

We made up this deal to show how the math works. It does not describe a real property or a real offer.

  • Purchase: $150,000 · Rehab: $40,000 · ARV: $260,000
  • Loan: 90% of the purchase plus 100% of the rehab = $175,000 at 9.99% interest only, 2 points, $1,500 in lender fees
  • Hold: 6 months, $550/mo for taxes, insurance, and utilities
  • Sell: 5% commission plus 1.5% closing costs

Running it through our calculator:

Item Amount
Interest (6 months) $8,741
Points + lender fees $5,000
Buying closing costs (2%) $3,000
Holding costs $3,300
Selling costs $16,900
Total project cost $226,941
Estimated profit $33,059
Cash you put in $35,041
ROI on cash 94.3% (about 189% annualized)

The catch: under the 70% rule, the max offer is $260,000 × 70% − $40,000 = $142,000. This deal pays $8,000 more than that and still pencils out, mostly because the lender covers so much of the cost. If the ARV comes in $25,000 low or the rehab runs over, that margin vanishes quickly. Treat the 70% rule as a smoke alarm, not a law. If a deal sets it off, recheck your ARV comps before you go higher.

Quick tip: ask how the rehab money is charged

Some lenders charge interest on the full rehab budget from day one. Others charge only on the draws you've actually taken. On a $40,000 rehab at about 10%, that difference can be worth $1,000 or more over six months. Ask before you sign. (Our calculator assumes the worst case, so your real number may come in better.)
