How hard money loan math works

Most fix-and-flip loans are interest-only: you pay interest each month and the whole balance at the end, when you sell or refinance. The lender funds part of the purchase at closing and holds the rehab money back, releasing it in draws after an inspector confirms the work is done.

  • Monthly payment = balance × rate ÷ 12.
  • Points = loan × points %. Paid once at closing (2 points on $184,000 = $3,680).
  • Max loan = the lower of (purchase × % + rehab × %) and (ARV × max %).
  • True annual cost = total cost ÷ loan ÷ months × 12. It shows what the loan costs per year once points and fees are spread over a short hold.

Worked example (EXAMPLE numbers, not a real deal or quote)

Buy for $160,000 with a $40,000 rehab and a $260,000 ARV. The lender covers 90% of purchase ($144,000) and all of the rehab ($40,000), so the loan is $184,000. That's under the 75%-of-ARV cap of $195,000, so the cost cap is the one that limits you. Terms: 10% interest-only, 2 points, $1,500 in fees, $250 per draw, 4 draws, held 7 months.

Questions to ask before you sign

  1. Is interest charged on the full loan or only on funds drawn?
  2. How many draws, what does each inspection cost, and how fast are they paid?
  3. What's every fee in dollars: origination, underwriting, processing, doc prep, wire?
  4. Is there a minimum interest period or prepayment penalty?
  5. What does an extension cost if the project runs long?

Common questions

How do you calculate a hard money loan payment?

Most hard money loans are interest-only. Monthly payment = loan balance × annual rate ÷ 12. At 10% on $184,000 that's $1,533 a month (example numbers). If the lender only charges interest on rehab money you've drawn, the payment starts lower and grows as draws are released.

What is Dutch interest on a hard money loan?

Dutch (or full-balance) interest means you pay interest on the whole loan, including rehab money the lender is still holding back, from day one. Non-Dutch means you only pay interest on funds actually drawn. Ask every lender which one they use; it can change your cost by hundreds or thousands of dollars.

How much does a hard money loan really cost?

Add interest over your whole hold, points, flat lender fees (origination, underwriting, processing, doc prep), draw inspection fees, and any extension fees. Divide by the loan amount and annualize it to compare against the note rate. Short holds with points usually cost a lot more per year than the rate suggests.

How much will a hard money lender lend?

Lenders usually cap the loan two ways: a percentage of purchase plus rehab (loan-to-cost) and a percentage of after-repair value (loan-to-ARV). You get whichever is lower. This calculator applies both caps so you can see which one limits you.

Are hard money rates negotiable?

Often, yes, mostly through experience tier, leverage, and points. Ask for a written quote showing rate, points, and every fee, then compare quotes with our points vs. rate calculator.
