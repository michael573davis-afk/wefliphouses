How BRRRR works

BRRRR stands for buy, rehab, rent, refinance, repeat. You buy a property that needs work, usually with a short-term hard money loan that funds part of the purchase and the rehab. Once the work is done and a tenant is in, you refinance into a long-term rental loan based on the new appraised value. The refi pays off the hard money loan, and if the value went up enough, some or all of your cash comes back out to use on the next deal.

What "cash left in the deal" means

It's the cash you put in (down payment, closing costs, points, interest, holding costs, and any rehab the lender didn't fund) minus what the refinance hands back. Zero or negative means you got all your money out. A positive number is what you still have tied up, and it's the number cash-on-cash return is measured against.

What is DSCR?

DSCR (debt service coverage ratio) is how most rental-property lenders check that the rent covers the loan. The common version is monthly rent ÷ monthly PITIA (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and HOA). A DSCR of 1.00 means the rent exactly covers the payment. Many DSCR lenders want 1.0 or higher, and some price better or lend more at 1.20–1.25 and up. Each lender sets its own minimums and formula, so confirm it before you count on a refi.

Things that break a BRRRR

  • The appraisal comes in low. Run the calculator with ARV 5–10% lower. If you'd be stuck with a lot of cash in the deal, the margin is thin.
  • Seasoning rules. Many lenders won't use the new appraised value until you've owned the property for a set number of months. Ask before you buy, and set "months until refinance" to match.
  • Rates move. A one-point higher refi rate can turn positive cash flow negative. Try a higher rate to see your cushion.
  • Rehab takes longer. Every extra month adds hard money interest and holding costs, and both come out of your cash.
